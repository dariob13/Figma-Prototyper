# Layout archetypes and verified page sequences

Use these as starting points when the brief does not already establish a structure. They are editorial suggestions, not official handbook templates. Select sections according to the citizen task; omit sections that do not help it. Inspect the live example before relying on it; record replacements if a URL redirects or disappears.

## Generic archetypes

### Information, reports, and departmental articles

Possible sequence: header, title and introduction, key facts when supported, readable article sections (text width ~718–792), related information (bullet link list), responsible contact, footer. Original example to verify: https://www.wien.gv.at/kontakt/klimaschutz-gesamtemissionen-gesunken. Do not invent statistics to fill a factbox.

### Portal or service hub

Possible sequence: header, icon tags, stage with CTA, priority service cards (grid m), quicklinks to frequent tasks, highlights, events, services carousel, contact + newsletter, footer. Prioritize common citizen tasks; news and campaigns must not displace the main service path without a reason from the brief.

### Urban development or infrastructure project

Possible sequence: header, project title and introduction, relevant visual (stage 8:3), verified status and dates, project details in cards, map, construction/traffic information (accordion), contact, footer. Original example to verify: https://www.wien.gv.at/verkehr/ringradweg-umgestaltung. Mark maps and status displays as simulated without live integration.

### Leisure, facilities, or topic guide

Follow the Baden or Film und Kino sequence above. Verify prices, opening hours, availability, and accessibility information; hero imagery, maps, tables, and live status displays are conditional choices.
